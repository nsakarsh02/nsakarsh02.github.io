DNOx PTC heater test fixture (Bosch)

ProblemAdBlue freezes at approximately −11°C, potentially restricting delivery from the DNOx supply module during cold starts. AdBlue supplies the ammonia required by the SCR catalyst to convert NOx emissions, primarily NO and NO₂, into nitrogen (N₂) and water.
SolutionDesigned and commissioned a dedicated test fixture to evaluate PTC heater sheets wrapped around the DNOx 2.2 supply-module filter housing across multiple heater-sheet and basebody configurations. Integrated a thermal imaging camera and data acquisition in a climatic chamber to assess hotspots, temperature distribution, current draw, and heat transfer; used fixture clamping-force calculations and FEA-based topology optimization to refine the mechanical design.
AchievementReduced fixture weight by 60% and cost by 35% through design optimization, while establishing a repeatable setup to compare heater configurations and assess electrothermal performance and instrumentation integrity.

Fuel-cell exhaust heating system (Bosch)

ProblemInvestigated the risk of water accumulation and freezing in a PEM fuel-cell vehicle's exhaust pipe during cold-weather operation. Using actual fuel-cell stack data, calculated water production under load at 851.868 g/min (14.2 mL/s) and, applying cathode humidifier and anode water-separator efficiencies, estimated approximately 13.5 L/h reaching the exhaust pipe.
SolutionDesigned a cylindrical exhaust-heating assembly with vertically arranged PTC elements, a thermally conductive heat-transfer body, insulated housing, and bus-bar connections. Also developed a three-sensor feedback architecture using ambient and exhaust inlet/outlet temperatures to regulate heater current, combining temperature-based control with the PTC elements' inherent self-regulating behavior.
AchievementUsing the estimated 13.5 L/h (3.75 mL/s) water flow, calculated a preliminary 627 W sensible-heating load for raising the water temperature from 60°C to 100°C, using the mass-flow rate, specific heat capacity, and 40°C temperature rise. This established a quantitative thermal-sizing basis and an integrated mechanical, electrical, and instrumentation design for subsequent prototype development and validation.
