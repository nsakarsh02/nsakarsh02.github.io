DNOx PTC heater test fixture

ProblemAdBlue (DEF) freezes at approximately −11°C, potentially restricting delivery from the DNOx supply module during cold starts. AdBlue supplies the ammonia the SCR catalyst needs to convert NOx, mainly NO and NO₂, into nitrogen (N₂) and water. The PTC heater needed electrical, thermal and functional validation under cold-start conditions.
SolutionDesigned and commissioned a dedicated test fixture to evaluate PTC heater sheets wrapped around the DNOx 2.2 supply-module filter housing across several heater-sheet and basebody configurations. Instrumented it with thermocouple arrays, 12 V current probes, a thermal imaging camera and DAQ in a climatic chamber to assess hotspots, temperature distribution, current draw and heat transfer through the insulated heater and aluminum housing. Used PicoScope waveform analysis and CAN monitoring to troubleshoot vibration-related signal disturbances with the engine running, and clamping-force calculations with FEA topology optimization to refine the design.
AchievementReduced fixture weight by 60% and cost by 35%, and established a repeatable DVP&R setup to compare heater configurations, characterize electrothermal response and check electrical connection and instrumentation integrity.

Bosch

Fuel-cell exhaust heating system

ProblemWater can accumulate and freeze in a PEM fuel-cell vehicle’s exhaust pipe in cold weather. From real stack data I calculated water production under load at 851.868 g/min (14.2 mL/s) and, applying cathode humidifier and anode water-separator efficiencies, estimated about 13.5 L/h reaching the exhaust pipe.
SolutionDesigned a cylindrical exhaust-heating assembly with vertically arranged PTC elements, a thermally conductive heat-transfer body, insulated housing and bus-bar connections. Added a three-sensor feedback architecture (ambient, exhaust inlet and outlet temperatures) to regulate heater current, combined with the PTC elements’ self-regulating behavior. A PTC element draws high current while cold, then its resistance rises with temperature and limits the current, which holds the heat output steady.
AchievementFrom the 13.5 L/h (3.75 mL/s) flow, calculated a preliminary 627 W sensible-heating load to take the water from 60°C to 100°C, using mass flow, specific heat and the 40°C rise. This gave a quantitative thermal-sizing basis and an integrated mechanical, electrical and instrumentation design for prototype development.

Test approach · NACS J3400

High-voltage distribution, from charge port to HV components

01 · Understand the power pathAC charge: charge port → OBC → charge contactors → battery pack. DC fast charge: charge port → HV connectors and cables → battery charging path (OBC bypassed). Power out: battery → contactors and pre-charge → HVJB, busbars and HV harness → inverter, DC/DC and HV loads.
02 · Where I would focus the testHVIL continuity and interlock status, safe contactor sequencing including pre-charge voltage convergence, IMD detection, voltage drop and contact resistance across busbar joints, connectors and other high-current interfaces, and checks for localized heating or intermittent faults during vibration and thermal tests. Connectors need an integrated thermal sensor and must pass bench tests that track contact temperature under load, with contact temperature kept below 100°C and temperature gradients forced during sensor validation.
03 · What I bring as a test engineerFixture design and validation test plans for regulation compliance and performance requirements (for example, J3400 DC charging contactors unintentionally closed during AC charging, connecting AC to DC). PicoScope and CANalyzer fault injection with hardware root-cause troubleshooting. DVP&Rs, repeatable procedures, and MATLAB and Python data analysis.
